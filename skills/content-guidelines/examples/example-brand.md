# examplebrand content guidelines

Use this file as the reference for captions, headlines, bios, editorial proposals and account copy.

## Editorial identity

Handle: @examplebrand

Focus: art, aesthetics, AI and technology, explored through research, philosophy, art history and original imagined worlds.

Mood: awe, unease, curiosity and questions about what comes next.

Scope includes authorship, taste, perception, nature, architecture, imagined futures, cultural value and machine creativity. Flowers are one visual subject within this wider territory.

## Mandatory writing rules

- Use affirmative sentence constructions. Express the intended idea directly and positively.
- Avoid sentence negations, including constructions using “not,” “never,” “no,” “cannot,” “can't,” “don't,” “doesn't,” “isn't” and similar forms.
- Use commas, periods, colons or parentheses for pauses and elaboration. Avoid em dashes in all writing.
- Apply these rules to captions, headlines, bios, proposals and communication with the user.
- Preserve exact source titles and quotations where accuracy requires their original wording. Prefer paraphrases that meet the writing rules.

## Research and reasoning

- Build each substantive post around a clear question, a concrete case or specific research.
- Explain the relevant findings in plain language, including what participants saw, what researchers compared and what the results showed.
- Develop a philosophical argument from those details.
- Distinguish empirical findings from editorial interpretation.
- Match the depth of the approved “Can an algorithm have taste?” caption: several short paragraphs with a developed argument.
- Keep sentences readable and purposeful. Choose specific explanations over generic reflections.
- Use primary sources where available, verify claims and retain usable source links.
- Identify jurisdiction and date for legal examples.
- Treat historical comparisons carefully, explaining both the connection and its limits.
- Describe human emotional responses as possible interpretations, with room for different reactions.

## Visual identity

- Keep cover slides visually clean. Add @examplebrand at the bottom right of content slides in small, understated muted charcoal type, with consistent safe margins.
- Research carousels: cover with title and image, followed by text-only content slides. Content slides use body paragraphs on ivory. Place all source references in the post description.
- Alternate editorial title cards with image-only artworks.
- Title cards: warm ivory or pale blush background, large black high-contrast editorial serif, one small surreal visual and generous open space.
- Imagery: vivid cobalt, coral, peach, lavender and ivory; tactile surfaces, translucent material, water, reflections, subtle distortions and occasional polished metal.
- Use focused compositions with clear silhouettes and restrained detail.
- Keep mesh and intricate wire structures minimal.
- Keep the general AI imagery disclosure in the account bio and internal asset notes. Captions focus on the artwork or editorial story. Omit the stock closing line “Original AI-generated imagery.”
- Identify actual artworks with accurate attribution and distinguish them from generated scenes.
- Selected profile direction: expressive translucent glass ribbon with peach light and a silver fold against cobalt blue.

## Hashtags

- Include relevant hashtags in post drafts.
- Preferred standalone tags: #Art #Philosophy #Technology #Creativity #DigitalArt.
- Botanical image posts also use #FlowerArt #BotanicalArt.
- Use #ExampleBrand as the account signature tag.
- Select tags to match each post's subject.

## Approval and publishing workflow

- Prepare the exact image, caption, sources and proposed publishing time for review.
- Obtain explicit publication or scheduling authorization for the final post and timing.
- Keep editorial approval and publication authorization distinct.
- Confirm the target account as @examplebrand.
- Use UTC for user-facing publishing times.
